MD5 Learning Experiment — Scientific Summary
================================================

Hypothesis: a neural net can learn MD5's structure well enough to
generalize to unseen plaintexts and accelerate preimage search.

Memorization probe (fit tiny train subset, test transfer):
  subset exact match   : 1.0000
  held-out exact match : 0.006667  (chance ≈ 0.001000)

Forward model (plain → hash bits) on held-out test:
  bit accuracy : 0.4992  (chance ≈ 0.50)
  exact match  : 0.000000  (chance ≈ 0)
  true avalanche   : 0.5031
  model avalanche  : 0.2687

Inverse model (hash → plain) :
  train exact match : 0.3300
  test  exact match : 0.006667  (chance ≈ 0.001000)
  test  char acc    : 0.0889  (chance ≈ 0.1000)
Search acceleration probe (rank of true plaintext):
  mean rank        : 540.7
  random mean rank : 500.5
  top-1 hit rate   : 0.0067

Verdict:
  Nets can memorize small MD5 lookup tables, but held-out recovery stays near chance: no evidence of learning an invertible MD5 structure or a search-accelerating flaw.

Caveat: this studies learnability under a tiny enumerable plaintext
space. It does not claim cryptanalytic breaks of MD5 at large.
